SCOPE PITCH-FIT MEASUREMENT - CONCLUSIONS
Image convention: x grows RIGHT, y grows DOWN.
Source: re-village-scope-vr/dev-archive/recon/2026-09-05-steering-sweep-flat/captures (1280x720 JPEG)

1. SCOPE DISC - LOCATED RELIABLY
   Outer scope picture: centre (636, 353), usable radius 258 px (bezel highlight fits at R=282).
   Inner dark sub-disc: centre ~(636, 353), radius ~92 px, grey rim out to ~105 px.
   Method: max-over-all-13-frames image, radial ray-casting from a seed centre to the last bright
   radius at 720 angles, least-squares circle fit. Residual rms 3.7 px (rim is a dodecagon).
   Geometry stable across all frames.
   TWO DISCS exist; both were measured. The outer annulus (105 < r < 258) carries the scene
   content and is the one the pitch series changes. A scene-only mask excluding crosshair, bezel
   and inner disc (114,398 px) was required - without it the correlator locks onto the static
   reticle and returns a spurious near-zero shift with high apparent confidence.

2. CAMERA DID NOT STAY PERFECTLY STILL
   Shroud region (r > 300, entirely outside the scope picture) vs baseline: residual shifts of
   3.6 to 17.1 px, unrelated to commanded angle. back-p180 residual 4.2 px.
   This is weapon idle sway moving the whole scope on screen: a +/-13 px noise floor on everything.
   It contaminates precision, not the conclusion.

3. WHAT WAS MEASURED, AND WHAT COULD NOT BE
   Straightforward translation measurement FAILED for pitch.
   Phase correlation (numpy FFT, high-pass sigma 6, raised-cosine radial window, parabolic
   sub-pixel) was validated on synthetic shifts of the baseline: exact to 0.01 px out to 150 px.
   On real pitch frames it returned peak/rms of 9-12 against a validated floor of ~68 - no match.
   An 80x80 scene-only patch-vote search over the whole frame confirmed: 0-3 percent of patches
   agree on any shift.
   Detection ceiling, established by shifting a real re-rendered frame (back-p180) by known
   amounts: this pipeline recovers coherent shifts up to ~300 px (58-97 percent consensus) and
   breaks at 400 px. The pitch series is therefore NOT a small shift the method missed.
   No px/deg slope could be produced. What could be measured without frame-to-frame overlap is
   the STRUCTURE-TENSOR ORIENTATION of the scene texture inside the disc.

4. PITCH SERIES (plane pitch 180 + d)
   Translation: no coherent shift at any step. Patch consensus 0,0,0,0,0 percent; median NCC 0.47
   (chance level for grass on grass); vote spread IQR 274-550 px in x, 158-322 px in y.
   Texture orientation (dominant gradient direction, deg from horizontal; Jxx/Jyy > 1 means
   upright reeds dominate):
        frame   deg   gradAngle   Jxx/Jyy
        base      0        3.6      1.761
        P+5       5       15.1      1.352
        P+10     10       17.2      1.573
        P+15     15       24.0      1.482
        P+20     20       42.4      1.047
        P+25     25       43.4      1.025
   Monotonic. Least-squares slope +1.68 deg of texture rotation per deg of plane pitch,
   residual rms 4.0 deg. This is a PROXY, not a pixel shift.
   Visually (strip-topband.png, strip-pitch.png): base shows upright reeds with distance visible
   past them; by P+25 the scope shows bare ground seen from above. No sky ever enters the picture.

5. YAW NULL CONTROL - GENUINELY FLAT
        frame    dx     dy   consensus   medNCC
        Y+5     +13     -5      100%      0.644
        Y+10     +1     +4      100%      0.718
        Y+15    +10      0      100%      0.683
        Y+20     -2     -3      100%      0.718
        Y+25    +10    -11      100%      0.852
   Every yaw frame is the SAME picture as the baseline, shifted only by the +/-13 px weapon sway,
   with no trend against angle. Texture orientation stays 1.8-2.9 deg (baseline 3.6) and Jxx/Jyy
   stays 1.62-1.85 (baseline 1.76). Yaw does nothing. The null control also proves the pipeline
   detects a match when one exists.

6. REPEATABILITY - GOOD
   back-p180 vs baseline: shift (-5, -10) px, 97 percent patch consensus, median NCC 0.60;
   texture orientation 1.5 deg vs 3.6, Jxx/Jyy 1.81 vs 1.76. The sweep returns to the baseline
   picture. Residual is the same +/-13 px sway seen in the yaw controls. Nothing drifted.

7. THE SIGN
   When the mirror plane is pitched by +5 deg, the scene inside the scope moves UP the image
   (toward smaller y) - that is, the view direction swings DOWN, toward the ground.
   Basis: across +5 to +25 the picture goes monotonically from upright reeds with distance
   visible past them, to bare ground viewed from above, and no sky ever enters the frame.
   Pitching the view up would have brought hazy distance and then sky down into the disc; it did
   the opposite.
   The direction is solid. THE MAGNITUDE IS NOT MEASURABLE from these screenshots - each 5 deg
   step replaces the picture entirely (> ~300 px equivalent, i.e. > ~60 px/deg), so no px/deg
   slope can be fitted. To get a number, re-shoot the sweep with steps of ~0.5 deg or finer.

8. CONFIDENCE, AND HOW THIS COULD BE WRONG
   Sign: high confidence. It rests on the monotonic ordering of six frames plus a flat null
   control and a clean return-to-baseline, not on one correlation peak.
   Magnitude: no result - reported as a bound only.
   Biggest reason it could be wrong: the sign is read from scene semantics (reeds standing up vs
   ground seen from above), not from a tracked feature. If the mirror render also ROLLS the view -
   and the texture orientation rotating by ~43 deg hints that it does, which a pure pitch would
   not produce - then "down" may be down along a rolled axis rather than down in screen space, and
   the pixel direction the picture actually travels could be off-axis. A finer sweep would settle
   both this and the magnitude at once.
