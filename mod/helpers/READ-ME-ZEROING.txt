===========================================================================
 ZEROING THE SCOPE  --  make it point where it shoots      2026-09-20
===========================================================================

 WHAT WE ARE DOING
 -----------------
 Zeroing moves the scope PICTURE so that whatever the crosshair sits on is
 what the bullet actually hits. It does not change where the rifle shoots.

 WHY IT PROBABLY NEEDS REDOING
 -----------------------------
 The current values (up 14.4, right 9.5) were measured on 13 September, with
 the mirror in its OLD position. On 18 September that position changed - the
 viewpoint moved from about a metre below your head to exactly at your eye.
 A zero measured before that change would now point wrong, and "lower and to
 the left" is exactly the sort of error it would give.

 So we are not adjusting a good zero. We are redoing one that was made for a
 different setup.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
 BEFORE STEP 1
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - The scope must already be working (START-SCOPE.bat, rifle in hands).
  - You need somewhere you can safely fire several shots and SEE where they
    land: a wall, a wooden post, a door. Snow and stone show hits well.
  - Stand still, a good distance back - 10 to 15 paces. Close up hides the
    error; far away makes it obvious.
  - You will need spare rifle ammo. If you are short, do this somewhere
    quiet and reload between tries.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
 PART 1  --  SEE HOW BAD IT REALLY IS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

 1. Double-click   ZERO-A-OFF.bat        (turns zeroing off completely)

 2. Look through the scope at your wall or post. Put the crosshair on
    something you can name - a knot in the wood, a bolt, a mark.

 3. FIRE ONE SHOT. Do not move.

 4. Look at where the hit actually landed, compared with the crosshair.

    TELL ME (or just remember): is the hit ABOVE or BELOW the crosshair,
    and is it LEFT or RIGHT of it? Roughly how far - a little, or right
    out of the scope picture?

 This is the raw error with no correction at all. It is the number
 everything else is measured from.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
 PART 2  --  COMPARE WITH THE OLD ZERO
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

 5. Double-click   ZERO-B-SHIPPED.bat    (the old 14.4 / 9.5 values)

 6. Same again: crosshair on the mark, fire one shot, look at where it hit.

 7. Is it BETTER or WORSE than with zeroing off?

    If it is WORSE, the old values are indeed fighting the new mirror pose,
    and we should build the zero up fresh from OFF.
    If it is BETTER but still out, we just need to nudge from here.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
 PART 3  --  NUDGE IT UNTIL IT IS RIGHT
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

 Work from whichever of the two was closer.

 The rule is simple: MOVE THE PICTURE TOWARDS WHERE THE BULLET WENT.

    hit was ABOVE the crosshair  ->  ZERO-UP.bat
    hit was BELOW the crosshair  ->  ZERO-DOWN.bat
    hit was RIGHT of the crosshair -> ZERO-RIGHT.bat
    hit was LEFT of the crosshair  -> ZERO-LEFT.bat

 8. Run the one that matches. Each click moves it 2 degrees - click twice
    for 4, three times for 6, and so on.

 9. Fire another shot at the same mark.

10. Repeat 8 and 9 until the hit sits on the crosshair.

    Go in big steps first (3 or 4 clicks) until you overshoot, then come
    back in single clicks. That is faster than creeping up on it.

11. When it looks right, MOVE SOMEWHERE ELSE and fire two or three more at
    a different distance. A zero that only works from one spot is not a
    zero - it has been fitted to that spot.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
 WHEN IT IS DONE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

 Tell me roughly what you ended up doing - "about six clicks up and two
 left from off", or "the old values were fine after all". I will read the
 exact numbers out of the log and write them in as the new defaults, so
 they survive a restart.

 ⚠ Until I do that, the values only last for this session.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
 IF SOMETHING IS ODD
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Picture streaks into dragged lines when your head points up or right:
    known, expected, and separate from this. Ignore it while zeroing -
    keep your head roughly facing the target.
  - The shot goes somewhere wildly unrelated, not just offset: stop and
    tell me. That is a different fault and nudging will not fix it.
  - You cannot see the hits at all: move closer, or try a snowy or wooden
    surface.
